#### 中文正文

一年前这听起来似乎不太可能，但如今，基于最新的人工智能模型并利用其发展趋势，实现 10 倍的产出提升已变得切实可行。结果是，同一公司内的不同团队，由于对人工智能的采用程度不同，产出水平也存在显著差异。

这与其他技术领域的现象截然不同。通常，在经济领域，人们会以公司为单位来衡量技术采用率，因为公司通常会采用相对标准化的方式来应用新技术，例如“该公司已实现数字化或采用云计算”。因此，各团队的提升幅度通常大同小异。

但人工智能领域的情况却大相径庭。即使在同一家公司内，人工智能采用程度最低和最高的团队，其所取得的成效也可能相差 10 倍。那些能够真正找到方法重新设计流程，从而在工作流程中最佳部署人工智能代理的团队，其生产力可能会提升一个数量级，具体取决于其工作职能。

目前这种情况主要集中在编码领域，但未来会有更多与编码相关的工作出现，因此我们可以预期其他领域也会出现类似的情况。这无疑会给公司内部带来一些剧烈的变化。

**This wouldn’t have sounded possible a year ago, but it’s becoming much more reasona…**

This wouldn’t have sounded possible a year ago, but it’s becoming much more reasonable now that you can get 10X output gains based on the latest in AI model and harness progress. The result is that teams within the same company are driving very different levels of output based on how they’ve adopted AI.

This is a different phenomenon from other tech. Typically in the economy you think of technology adoption rates on a per firm basis, because the company generally adopts a relatively standardized approach to doing the new tech thing. Like “that company has gone digital or adopted the cloud”. So generally the uplift across teams is more or less the same across teams. 

AI is starting to look quite different. Even within the same company you can have a 10X delta between the lowest and highest adopters of AI and the impact they’re seeing. The teams that are able to actually figure out how to redesign their processes to best deploy agents in their workflows can see up to an order of magnitude jump in productivity depending on the job function.

Right now this is heavily concentrated in coding, but more work will become coding adjacent so we can expect similar results elsewhere too. This is going to definitely lead to some wild dynamics within companies.

**America can build the best frontier models and still lose the global AI race.**

America can build the best frontier models and still lose the global AI race. Which is why Jensen’s return matters far beyond Nvidia.

Governments around the world want sovereign AI: models they can run on their own soil, on their own infrastructure, under their own control.

If American companies won’t meet that demand, Chinese companies will. Countries will start with Chinese models, then adopt Chinese infrastructure and chips. Having the smartest model won’t mean much if most of the world runs on someone else’s stack.

We’ve seen a version of this play out with Huawei. China understood that supplying the world’s telecom infrastructure creates lasting strategic influence. Once a country builds its networks around your equipment, it also builds technical expertise, supplier relationships, and future investment around it. Replacing that equipment becomes expensive and disruptive (the US government is now trying to finance replacement operations but it’s been difficult).

That’s why Jensen’s backing of the American open-model ecosystem is so important. Open models give startups the freedom to compete and give countries a reason to build on American technology.
