趋同

当每个人都以他人为参照来决定创作什么时，我们最终会停止创作任何属于自己的东西。

我们复制成功之作，借用语言、美学和功能。我们称之为向市场学习、遵循最佳实践、保持竞争力。最终，一切都变得似曾相识，精致完美，可以互换，没有瑕疵，也失去了生命力。

均值本身并非坏事。当我们抹去一切差异化的理由时，所有事物最终都会归于均值。

数字比情感更容易比较，所以我们创作能够产生数字的东西。我们研究什么能吸引眼球，然后围绕它塑造自己。最终，反馈不再指导作品，而是反过来塑造作品。我们不再问自己想说什么，而是问说了什么会得到回报。

人工智能让这个循环变得无比顺畅。它可以汲取比我们一生所能接触到的还要多的参考资料。但能够接触到所有视角并不等同于拥有一个视角。它没有经历过特定的人生，因此无法体会某些细节令人难以忍受，而另一些细节却美妙绝伦。当我们要求它替我们做选择，而不带任何我们自己的意图时，我们得到的往往是看似合理、易于认可的方案。

然后我们发布、评估、复制，再将其反馈到下一轮。一股中间路线的浪潮席卷而来。

这不仅改变了作品本身，也改变了创作者。

我们用平台可以分发、市场可以理解的类别来描述自己：设计师、创始人、创造者。选择一个细分领域，打造品牌，保持一致性。那些不符合这些类别的部分会变得碍事，即使这些矛盾之处可能孕育出我们最有趣的作品。我们变得更容易被归类，却更难被区分开来。

我认为我们需要像艺术家那样去创作。

艺术家通过作品发展出独特的视角：创作、观察、选择、失败、回归。即使无人问津，他们也能从中领悟真正重要的东西。技艺赋予这种视角一种足够精确的形式，让其他人也能体验到。

作品之外的生活同样重要。你生活过的地方，你爱过的人，你不好意思承认的那些事。这些并非让你无话可说的干扰因素，而是你表达想法的途径。

我们对音乐、绘画、摄影、电影都深有体会。我们期待创作者参与其中，体验那些我们原本无法接触到的事物。

软件也能做到这一点。

一款软件表达了人们对生活应有感受的信念：什么值得你关注，什么应该让你自由改变，一项任务应该像文书工作还是游戏。即使是微小的交互也能承载着创作者的关怀、幽默或好奇心。

但我们很少将软件视为一种媒介，而是将其视为一个行业。需要开拓市场，需要优化工作流程，需要追赶竞争对手。我们添加某个功能，仅仅因为别人已经拥有，却从未质疑它是否真的必要。产品不断堆砌相同的组件，最终失去了最初吸引用户的特质。

抛开品牌包装，它们本质上都是一样的。

艺术家也会从他人身上学习。问题在于，你是将这些影响吸收进自己的观点，还是利用它们来逃避形成自己的观点。

你想让别人感受到什么、理解什么或做什么？为什么是这种特定的形状？你注意到了哪些现有工具似乎忽略的细节？

这些问题的答案无法从竞争对手的产品路线图中直接复制。

如果我们几乎可以制造任何东西，那么决定什么才值得存在就变得更加重要，而不是不那么重要。速度赋予我们更多运用判断力的机会，但这并不意味着我们可以免除责任。

我不希望每个人都成为相同想法的更高效的传播者。我希望人们利用这些工具，深入探索他们真正关心的事情，让作品足够具体，让我们能够感受到其背后的创作者。

如果我们最终都创造出同样的东西，那么每个人都能创造东西的意义何在？

**convergence to the mean when everyone looks at everyone else to decide what to make…**

convergence to the mean

when everyone looks at everyone else to decide what to make, we eventually stop making anything of our own.

we copy what works. borrow the language, the aesthetic, the features. we call it learning from the market, following best practices, staying competitive. together, everything starts to feel familiar. polished, interchangeable. nothing wrong. nothing alive.

the mean isn’t necessarily bad. it’s where everything lands when we remove the reasons for it to be different.

numbers are easier to compare than feelings, so we make things that produce numbers. we study what gets attention, then shape ourselves around it. eventually the feedback stops informing the work and starts authoring it. we’re no longer asking what we want to say. we’re asking what will be rewarded for saying.

ai makes this loop frictionless. it can draw from more references than we could see in a lifetime. but access to every perspective isn’t the same as having one. it hasn’t lived a particular life that makes one detail unbearable and another beautiful. when we ask it to choose for us without bringing any intention of our own, we get something plausible, easy to approve.

then we ship it, measure it, copy it, and feed it back into the next round. a tidal wave of mid.

this changes not just the work, but the people making it.

we describe ourselves in categories platforms can distribute and markets understand. designer. founder. creator. pick a niche. build a brand. be consistent. the parts that don’t fit become inconvenient, even when those contradictions might produce our most interesting work. we become easier to classify and harder to tell apart.

i think we need to approach making things more like artists.

an artist develops a perspective through the work. making, noticing, choosing, failing, returning. learning what matters even when nobody else cares. craft gives that perspective a form precise enough for someone else to experience.

the life outside the work matters too. the places you’ve lived, the people you’ve loved, the things you’re embarrassed to care about. these aren’t distractions from having something to say. they’re how you come to have something to say.

we understand this with music, paintings, photographs, movies. we expect a person to be present in the choices. we want to experience something we wouldn’t have encountered without them.

software can do this too.

a piece of software expresses beliefs about how life should feel. what deserves your attention. what you should be free to change. whether a task should feel like paperwork or play. even a small interaction can carry someone’s care, humor, or curiosity.

but we rarely talk about software as a medium. we talk about it as an industry. markets to capture, workflows to optimize, competitors to catch up with. we add a feature because someone else has it, without asking whether it belongs. products accumulate the same parts and lose what made them worth choosing.

underneath the branding, it’s all the same thing.

artists learn from others too. the question is whether you’re absorbing those influences into a point of view, or using them to avoid developing one.

what are you trying to let someone feel, understand, or do? why this particular shape? what have you noticed that the existing tools don’t seem to notice?

those questions don’t have answers you can borrow from a competitor’s roadmap.

if we can make almost anything, deciding what deserves to exist becomes more important, not less. speed gives us more chances to exercise judgment. it doesn’t remove the responsibility.

i don’t want a world where every person becomes a more efficient distributor of the same ideas. i want people to use these tools to go further into what they actually care about. to make things specific enough that we can feel someone behind them.

what is the point of everyone being able to make something, if we all end up making the same thing?

#### 中文正文

目前人工智能代理的应用仍然呈现两极分化的趋势。一方面是编码和编码相关任务，这些领域的应用已经蓬勃发展；另一方面则是其他领域。

即使在编码领域，应用模式也呈现出非常大的差异：可能只有一小部分开发者部署了后台代理，用于并行处理项目；而其余开发者仍然采用一对一的代理工作模式。

此外，在其他知识工作领域，人工智能代理的真正应用仍处于起步阶段。原因在于，大多数工作流程仍需重新设计才能与代理协同工作。

这与部署聊天系统以提高研究效率截然不同，它需要重建工作流程，以新的方式连接数据，建立新的决策问责机制（尤其是在代理参与的情况下），改变治理和合规范式，进行安全升级等等。

目前，除了少数几个特定领域的应用之外，我们对人工智能代理的未来发展方向仍然知之甚少。这就是为什么我们可以预期，代理采用率将比我们目前所看到的增长100倍。

<details><summary>英文原文</summary>

**AI agent adoption is still very bimodal right now.**

AI agent adoption is still very bimodal right now. You have coding and coding adjacent tasks which have taken off, and then everything else. 

And even within coding, you have a very wide continuum of adoption patterns, with some likely a small percentage of developers deploying background agents working on projects in parallel, and the rest of everyone else still working with agents 1:1. 

Then, there’s the entire rest of knowledge work where real agentic adoption is still very early. The reason for this is that most of the workflows still need to reengineered to work with agents. 

This isn’t the same as deploying a chat system for better research efficiency, but instead requires workflows to be rebuilt, data to be wired up in new ways, new practices for accountability and liability of decisions when agents are in the mix, governance and compliance paradigm changes, security upgrades, and more. 

We’re still so unbelievably early in what this is going to look like outside of a few categories of work right now. This is why you can basically expect 100X more agent adoption from what we’ve seen so far.
