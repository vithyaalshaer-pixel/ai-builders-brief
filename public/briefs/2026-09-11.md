#### 中文正文

这是一份非常优秀且设计精良的报告。

然而，其中有两个重要的假设，一旦你开始深入研究，就会发现它们会显著改变分析结果：

1. 报告假设工作可以分解为若干任务。

2. 报告将自动化创造的人类任务始终视为自动化任务的一部分。换句话说，其基本假设是自动化总是减少人类劳动（或者充其量保持不变）。

反过来思考：

自动化总是减少人类劳动吗？

以我的经验来看，答案是否定的。自动化往往会为人类创造更多工作。

我在这里（https://t.co/Lb7SUCe2pO）详细论证了为什么这在结构上是正确的。但我认为，任何对人工智能经济影响的客观分析都必须认真对待工作创造问题。

工作可以分解为若干任务吗？

是的，这是一个有用的视角。但任务列表是看待工作的一种方式——它使得“有多少工作可以自动化”这个问题变得切实可行。因此，它是有用的。

但这并非全貌，它只是我们提问方式下呈现的工作状态。

举个例子：在这种模式下，新任务从何而来？仅仅是老板把任务清单添加到员工的任务列表中吗？这些任务又从何而来？除非你能很好地模拟这一点，否则你的分析就遗漏了一个关键要素（参见上文第一点）。

另一种看待这个问题的方式，借鉴维特根斯坦和海德格尔的观点，是工作是一种看待和关注世界的特定方式。编辑会注意到护士注意不到的事情，反之亦然。你关心什么，就会影响你的观察、什么事值得做，以及什么才算做得好。你可以把其中一些明确地列成任务清单。但随着你的工具和环境的变化，同样的关注点也可能衍生出全新的任务。这个过程本身就是工作的一部分。

以上内容在本次分析中完全缺失（这可以理解，因为顾名思义，它很难建模！）。

但如果您认真对待它，它应该会极大地改变您对自动化和就业的看法。

**this is a very good and well designed report however, two impt assumptions in it th…**

this is a very good and well designed report

however, two impt assumptions in it that significantly change the analysis once you start to play with it:

1. the report assumes that jobs can be decomposed into tasks
2. the report models human tasks created by automation always as a fraction of automated tasks. in other words, the underlying assumptions is automation always reduces human labor (or at best leaves it the same)

taken in reverse order:

does automation always reduce human work?
in my experience, the answer is no. automation often creates multiples more work for humans. 

i have a full argument here (https://t.co/Lb7SUCe2pO) for why this is structurally true. but i think any honest analysis of the economic impacts of AI has to take work creation seriously. 

can jobs be decomposed into tasks?
yes, that is a useful lens. but lists of tasks is a way of looking at jobs—one that makes the question of how much of a job is automatable actually tractable. therefore, it's useful.

but this is not the full picture, it's just jobs as exposed to our method of questioning. 

a good example: under this model, where do the new tasks come from? are they just from bosses that add a list to the task list for workers to complete? where do those tasks come from? unless you can model that well you are missing a key term in the analysis (see the first point above.)

another way of looking at this, borrowing from wittgenstein and heidegger, is that a job is a particular way of seeing and caring about the world. an editor notices things a nurse doesn’t, and vice versa. what you care about shapes what you notice, what seems worth doing, and what counts as doing it well. you can make some of that explicit as a task list. but as your tools and circumstances change, the same cares can generate entirely new tasks. that process is part of the job.

the above is completely missing from this analysis (understandably because it's, by definition, very hard to model!) 

but if you take it seriously, it shoudl substantially change your perspective automation and jobs
